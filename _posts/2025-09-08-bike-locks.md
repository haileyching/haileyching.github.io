In Cambridge, if you own a bike, you definitely also own a lock. If you don't, the statement will still hold true because soon you will cease to own a bike.

Bike locks are a crucial object to prevent theft. But do you know that some bike thiefs use them to steal bikes? My friend locked his bike in a university site, and after lectures a very curious second lock appeared on it. Staff said they are not allowed to help him remove it, so he went home by foot and hoped that the owner of that second lock will remove it some time.

They certainly did remove the lock - along with the bike.

Crazy! We've been using bike locks against bike theft and now they are using it against us. How does a bike lock even work? What is the philosophy of a bike lock?

I think a bike lock reduces the appeal and chances of the bike being stolen, by increasing the time needed and difficulty of theft. Of course, I'm not a bike thief myself so I am not qualified to comment on their psychology. But if the bike thief indeed uses a lock themselves, they would not only level the playing field by symmetrizing the situation, but more so in their favor because it is their specialty to break through locks, not ours. They also get to decide the time of strike (敵在暗我在明). Not gonna lie, how clever.

Note that the lock reduces the *appeal* of theft. This is because they must remove the bike before someone catches onto what they are doing, or before the owner comes back. Perhaps the foolproof way to reduce the appeal is to buy a older, less fancy bike. I don't really know what I would do if my bike got locked by someone like that.
