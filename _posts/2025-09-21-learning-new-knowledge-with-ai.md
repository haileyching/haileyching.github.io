# Learning New Knowledge with AI

In fact, I was learning AI with AI yesterday. I really should make another post on how AI has changed coding, but let's skip that for now.

I'm quite interested in the topic of swarm intelligence for a while and I decided yesterday I'll be a hermit in my room and speedrun a product with AI. Perhaps some visualization of a successful model.

It turns out that I didn't do much - barely started learning what is Reinforcement Learning - but I did figure a good ton on how AI should be used for learning stuff like this.

Initially I started by telling it I want to speedrun the topic in 8 hours and make an end result. I already know that AI is incredibly powerful in researching and organizing a lot of content. So I told it about my goals and asked it for a roadmap and schedule what I should be learning today. So it would roughly be 3 phases:

1. 9AM-12PM swarm intelligence concepts
2. 1PM-3PM machine learning principles
3. 3-6PM implementation 

which I thought was completely fine. 

Initially: Phase 1, I think the morning was like, we go through the syllabus we created and Claude will give explain the concepts and cite the relevant source material. After showing me that, I can prompt it to write down in my obsidian vault directly
Change 1: Source materials is good in the sense that (a) it allows me to check against hallucinations and (b) I'm sometimes more interested in how the paper presents its findings (c) there are more diagrams. 
Change 2: I prefer learning content with a narrative or a single source to start with, so Wikipedia was better. 
Change 3: I always end up finding side quests or side questions which I want to look into, so I write them down on a paper instead (because its still pretty bad at keeping track of questions)
Improvement: Perhaps for some new fields of research, I can ask it to shape a narrative for me (BUT THERE'S ALMOST ALWAYS WIKIPEDIA??)

Expectation: Phase 2, learning machine learning - it went straight at throwing a concept called Markov Decision Process at me, and value-function etc. And then throws python code that I can use immediately
Change 1: I decided that if I want to learn things properly, and build a solid foundation, then there's no point rushing for a result in one day. So to focus on consolidating concepts, once again I switched to a very good youtube video.
Change 2: To consolidate computer science concepts I guess it's best to actually write the code from the very scratch if it is a simple example, like the grid navigation RL. Otherwise if you just let AI do all of it you can't build up that mental environment for development.(This I'm not sure - maybe you do want to use libraries or ask it to debug, but you shouldn't just let AI write it all after you input a high level structure or idea. In learning the fundamentals, details probably matter a bit more???)

    - With AI being a "blackbox", it becomes really important to grasp a strong conceptual understanding, UNLESS you are going to the other extreme: to completely trust the blackbox just because it can reliably deliver results
    - This creates a skill gap. AI is only going to get better and better, so there will be a gap dominated by AI, between entry level (trust fully) and advanced level. There is no more intermediate. note: by entry level I mean the baseline of what AI can do, because everyone can use it. I don't mean true entry level of what a human can do.
    - Basically, if you're a specialist, you can go for mastering a skill that AI can't quite do yet and don't let it catch up to you. For all other skills, you can trust the results of AI and outsource to it. But it will be harder for the new entry level to further advance if they are used to the comfort of AI and never learn the foundations required for further progress. So they will simply need to wait for AI.
